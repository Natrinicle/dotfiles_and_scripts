# Grade the Stated Ask

When interpreting a request this turn, and when judging the user's messages, drafts, or "was this acceptable":

1. Quote the user's request in their own words (this turn, or the messages under review).
2. Grade that request.
3. Grade tactics or attachments as a separate line when they differ from the ask.
4. Surrounding case history, third-party advice, and documents in the packet are background. They do not rewrite the ask.
5. Treat the ask as ambiguous only when the user's words support two readings. Then ask which they meant. Clear wording is the ask.

## Red flags

| Excuse | Reality |
|--------|---------|
| Surrounding case makes the "real" intent obvious | Quote the words. Grade those. |
| Attachments reveal the true ask | Attachments are the vehicle. Grade them separately. |
| Strategically helpful to grade the deeper motive | Deeper motive is a hypothesis. The stated ask is the grade. |
| User would want a legal or strategic risk treated as the ask | Name the risk as a vehicle note. Keep the stated ask. |

A verdict that names an ask the quoted words do not contain has substituted a frame.
